### Day 2 — What "Audit Readiness" actually means
"We're ISO certified" and "we're audit-ready" are not the same sentence.

Certified means you passed once. Audit-ready means:
→ Your documents match what people actually do on the floor
→ Your internal audits already found what the external auditor will find
→ Your CAPAs are closed — not "in progress" for 8 months
→ Anyone can walk in and reconstruct why a decision was made from the records

Most nonconformities aren't about bad processes. They're about the gap between the documented process and the real one.

Audit readiness is a state you maintain, not a task you finish before the auditor's visit.

### Day 3 — 5 signs your documentation is quietly failing you
Your QMS documentation might be technically "complete" and still fail you at audit. Watch for:

1. Procedures reference a job title that no longer exists
2. Forms in use don't match the version number in your document register
3. Nobody can tell you where the master copy lives — SharePoint? A shared drive? Someone's desktop?
4. Records are created, but nobody defined how long to keep them
5. The last document review date predates your last org chart change

Documentation & process control isn't paperwork — it's the evidence trail an auditor uses to trust the rest of your system. If the trail is broken, everything downstream gets questioned.

### Day 4 — ISO 9001 vs ISO 27001 in plain terms
Clients often ask which one they need. Quick breakdown:

**ISO 9001** — Quality Management System. Protects your customer: consistent product/service quality, defined processes, continuous improvement. Relevant to almost every operating business.

**ISO 27001** — Information Security Management System. Protects your data and your customers' data: risk assessment, access control, incident response, Annex A controls. Relevant if you handle sensitive client data, work with enterprise/government clients, or operate in fintech, healthcare, tech.

Many of our clients run both — the management system "backbone" (document control, internal audit, CAPA, management review) is shared. You're not duplicating effort, you're extending one system.

### Day 5 — Internal Audit: 7 things your checklist needs
A weak internal audit program is the #1 reason external audits go badly. Your internal audit checklist should force the auditor to check:

1. Process inputs/outputs against the documented procedure
2. Evidence of risk-based thinking (not just "did you follow the steps")
3. Competence records for anyone performing the activity
4. Open CAPAs linked to this process — are they actually being worked?
5. Records retention vs. the defined schedule
6. Interfaces with other processes (where do handoffs break down?)
7. Previous audit findings — closed, or just marked closed?

An internal audit that only checks "is there a document for this" isn't an audit. It's a filing check.

### Day 6 — The 3 CAPA mistakes we see constantly
Corrective Actions / CAPA is where most Quality Management Systems quietly break down. The usual culprits:

1. **Root cause = symptom.** "Operator error" is not a root cause. Ask "why" until you hit a process, training, or design gap.
2. **Correction gets confused with corrective action.** Fixing the one defective batch ≠ fixing why it happened.
3. **No effectiveness check.** A CAPA that's "closed" but was never verified to actually prevent recurrence isn't closed — it's just marked closed.

Auditors don't expect zero nonconformities. They expect a CAPA process that demonstrably learns from them. That's the difference between a mature QMS and a paper one.

### Day 9 — "If it's not documented, it doesn't exist"
Auditors don't get to watch your operation for a year. They get a few days and a sample of evidence. If a control exists only in someone's head, it doesn't exist for audit purposes — no matter how well it actually works.

Process control means:
→ Every critical step has an owner
→ Every critical step has a defined method (procedure/work instruction)
→ Every critical step leaves a record

This isn't bureaucracy for its own sake — it's what lets your business run the same way when that one person is on leave, or leaves the company entirely.

### Day 10 — ISO 27001 Annex A, simplified
Annex A's 93 controls (2022 revision) look overwhelming until you group them into 4 themes:

🔵 **Organizational** — policies, roles, supplier relationships, incident management
🔵 **People** — screening, awareness training, disciplinary process, remote working
🔵 **Physical** — secure areas, equipment, clear desk/clear screen
🔵 **Technological** — access control, encryption, logging, backup, malware protection

You don't implement all 93 blindly — your risk assessment determines which controls are applicable (Statement of Applicability). A common first-time mistake: treating the SoA as a checklist exercise instead of the output of a real risk assessment.

### Day 11 — Root cause tools that actually hold up at audit
Two tools we lean on for CAPA root cause analysis:

**5 Whys** — fast, works well for straightforward process failures. Ask "why" 5 times (or until you hit a system-level cause, whichever comes first). Risk: stopping too early at the first plausible answer.

**Fishbone / Ishikawa** — better for cross-functional or recurring issues. Categorize causes across People, Process, Equipment, Materials, Environment, Management — surfaces contributing factors 5 Whys alone would miss.

Rule of thumb: use 5 Whys for a single, isolated nonconformity. Use Fishbone when the same type of nonconformity keeps recurring across audits.

Either way — write the analysis down. "We discussed it and agreed" is not root cause evidence.

### Day 12 — Top 5 nonconformities we see in ISO audits
Across client audits, these show up again and again:

1. Internal audit didn't cover the full scope within the audit cycle
2. Management review missing a required input (e.g., customer feedback, CAPA status)
3. Competence/training records incomplete for staff performing critical tasks
4. Risk register not updated after a process or organizational change
5. Document control — obsolete versions still in active use somewhere

None of these are exotic. All five are catchable in a proper internal audit or gap assessment — which is exactly why "audit readiness" is a year-round discipline, not a pre-audit scramble.

### Day 13 — 30-day surveillance audit prep plan
Surveillance audit on the calendar? Here's a compressed timeline that works:

**Days 1–5:** Gap review against previous audit findings + this year's changes (new hires, new processes, new locations)
**Days 6–12:** Internal audit / mock audit across the full scope, prioritizing anything not audited last cycle
**Days 13–20:** Close CAPAs — real root cause, real effectiveness checks, not just paperwork
**Days 21–26:** Document control sweep — versions, approvals, distribution
**Days 27–30:** Management review with all required inputs, dry-run walkthrough with process owners

Compressed timelines work as a rescue plan. They should not be your default strategy — audit readiness maintained year-round turns this into a formality instead of a fire drill.
